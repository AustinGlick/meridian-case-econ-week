## 0. Recommendation first (half a page)

- Recommend **(b) make applicants bear it**: re-time the situational section, disable paste, and
  flag sub-15-minute answers for recruiters. Cost against the status quo as a **range** per
  retained employee and per year (E19), excluding and including operational quality (E17).
- One-sentence reason: the section has stopped screening on the new system (E12, E12b, E12d),
  the timed system keeps a usable share of the signal (E12b/E12d legacy 2023+ cells), and the
  timed centers retain more hires today (E01b, E17). Every other option's retention effect is an
  assumption; (b)'s is observed.
- The biggest hole, named: the seven timed centers are small and their own signal is eroding
  (E07b, E12e), so "copy the legacy configuration" is an upper bound; the true gain lies between
  the DiD estimate and the legacy-observed gap (E19 rows b-low / b / b-high).

## 1. (a) What the process gets right and where it fails (one page)

- Gets right: license, referral, resume and the structured interview still predict retention in
  every cell and are stronger in the AI era (E11, E11b, E14). The volume machine works: openings
  fill, applications arrive (E10).
- Fails, judgment 1: the screen still weights the essay exactly as it did when the essay worked
  (E11c: fixed weight, R-squared ~0.85). The process has not adapted.
- Fails, judgment 2: recruiter capacity is spread thinner every year (E10, E10b, E10c); the new
  ATS draws more applications per opening in the AI era (E10d), so the least informative essays
  arrive where recruiters have the least time to read them.
- Consequence: retention falls more on the new ATS after 2023 (E01, E01b), reopen rates rise
  (E02, E02b as a range), and separations rise in every reason category (E03, E03c). Whitfield's
  "best-run centers are worst" is the pilot-wave effect (E04, E05).

## 2. (b) Does the situational section still work as a screen, and how we know (1.5 pages)

- **Comparison**: slope of essay score on the six-month outcome, within each of four cells
  (legacy/new ATS x application before/after 2023-01). **On whom**: permanent hires (selected
  on the essay, attenuated) and flex placements (not selected on the essay by Meridian).
- Result: E12 (bins), E12b (hires retention), E12c (hires reopen), E12d (flex reopen). Signal
  retained by cell as a range across the two samples: E15e.
- Mechanism: E13, E13b, E13c — slow essays still work everywhere; fast high scores on the new
  ATS in the AI era predict worse retention; effort no longer earns points.
- Placebo: E14 — every other input holds or strengthens.
- Timing and identification: E06 (scores climb from 2023 in all new-ATS centers regardless of
  migration date, E06b), E15 (no pre-trend), E12e (within-center before/after), E16 (not the
  labor market), E15c (not range restriction), E15d (survives wild-cluster bootstrap).
- **What must be true** for this to mean the essay stopped screening: (i) flex placement is not
  selected on the essay (case text); (ii) nothing unobserved changed at migrating centers in 2023
  that also changed essay validity (E15, pilot-vs-never pre-period parity in H9); (iii) the
  2023-01 cutoff is substantively right — the rise is a ramp, so any cutoff in 2023 gives the
  same story (E06b).
- Could not determine: whether any individual used AI; what the ~94% of unreviewed applicants
  look like; the legacy system's own erosion path beyond 2025.
