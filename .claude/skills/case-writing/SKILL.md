# Structure (four pages maximum)
1. **Recommendation first** (half a page). Which of the four options, the cost range per
   retained employee and per year against the status quo, the one-sentence reason, and the
   biggest hole in the analysis. A reader who stops here knows what to do.
2. **(a) The process** (one page). What works, what fails, as a judgment supported by named
   exhibits. Not a list of observations.
3. **(b) The situational section** (one and a half pages). State explicitly: the comparison
   being made, on whom, the regimes, and what would have to be true for the comparison to mean
   what we say. Give the range of signal loss (hires sample vs flex sample) and why it is a
   range. The mechanism (time in section). The placebo. The pilot-center timing argument.
4. **(c) The decision** (one page). The four options costed in one table, the sensitivity,
   the three "I would recommend something else if …" sentences, and Round 2 triggers.

# Rules from the prompt
- Every number in the text cites an exhibit id in the appendix. No number without a home.
- Ranges, not point estimates, with the reason they are ranges.
- Say plainly what could not be determined.
- Do not confuse robustness work with deliverables: it supports the three answers.
- The team owns every number: no "the model said so." Write so any teammate can defend it.

# Style
- Plain English; define technical terms once, in one line.
- Active voice, short sentences. No bullet lists in the narrative body except the final
  "what would change our recommendation" sentences.
- No filler ("it is worth noting"), no hedging stacks ("may possibly suggest").
- Spell-check before returning. British or American spelling, consistently, not both.

# Sourcing numbers
Every figure in the draft is copied from `outputs/RESULTS.md` or the exhibit table, never
recalled from memory. If a number is not in RESULTS.md, run the script first.
